## 2. Intended purpose

EspHealth is intended to enable patients to record, organise and follow health-related information over time, including symptoms, health indicators, medication or treatment entries, questionnaire responses, quality-of-life information and personal health goals. It provides views of the recorded information, educational resources, reminders and controlled sharing to support patients’ understanding of their health and communication with authorised care or support providers.

Through the coach/healthcare-professional interface, authorised users can review information for the patients assigned to them, follow changes over time and receive notifications generated by configured rules, including potential deterioration signals. These outputs support review and follow-up. **Whether, and how, particular outputs are intended to guide diagnosis or treatment decisions remains to be confirmed.** The final clinical purpose, expected user response and supporting evidence for those uses are recorded before this document is approved.

EspHealth can be used as a personal health journal or within a configured care, coaching or research workflow. Each configuration identifies its purpose, enabled functions and permitted users. A configuration’s use conditions supplement this product-level description.

## 3. Functional scope, inputs and outputs

The table describes intended functions. Availability and verification for a particular release are recorded in the product and configuration evidence.

| Function | Main inputs | Main outputs / intended use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Health journal and questionnaires | Patient entries about symptoms, health indicators, medication/treatment use, quality of life and other configured questionnaires; permitted assisted entries | Stored entries, histories, charts and questionnaire results that help patients and authorised users follow recorded information over time |
| Goals, education and engagement | Patient goals, selected educational content, schedules and configured profile or content-selection rules | Goal tracking, educational content, reminders and support messages |
| Sharing and coach/HCP review | Patient records, sharing choices, user permissions and patient assignments | Permitted patient-data views and summaries for authorised recipients, supporting communication, review and follow-up |
| Rule-based notifications | Recorded values or scores and configured trigger rules | Notifications to the configured authorised recipients, including signals of potential deterioration for review |
| Paper-journal and connected-data capture | Captured paper entries, supported wearable data and other permitted external sources | Digital records, imported observations and information about their source; optical character recognition (OCR) converts paper entries into data for the relevant review workflow |
| External analysis and reporting, where included | Data or results received from specified external programs, including scores or predictive analyses | Displayed results, configured content triggers or authorised reports/exports; the purpose and interpretation of each external result are defined for that integration |

Medication and treatment entries describe recorded information; the intended role of any output in changing treatment remains part of the clinical-purpose decision. A rule-generated signal is not itself a confirmed clinical event.

The wider application also includes supporting functions such as calendars, care-provider references, personal timelines and clinical-trial browsing. Their interfaces and any contribution to a medical-purpose workflow are considered in the product scope assessment; inclusion in the application does not by itself establish a diagnostic or treatment claim.

## 4. Intended indications and patient population

EspHealth is designed to be tailored across **all health conditions and age groups**. This is the broad scope of its configuration capability, rather than a claim that one unchanged configuration is suitable for every patient.

The population for each configuration identifies the relevant condition or health context, age range, selection criteria, instruments, language and accessibility needs. It also identifies whether use is independent or assisted. These details connect the intended use to the relevant risk, usability and performance evidence.

Patients using the application independently need to be able to understand the relevant instructions and complete the required interactions. Where assisted use is included, the configuration defines the authorised helper’s role, permitted entries and access. Use by children or people needing cognitive, literacy or other assistance is addressed through the relevant configuration and usability assessment.

Condition-specific indications, exclusions and contraindications follow that assessment. A general statement that there are no contraindications has not been established for all configurations.

## 5. Intended users

| User group | Intended activities and use conditions |
| --- | --- |
| Patients | Record and view their own information, complete configured questionnaires, access content and reminders, manage goals and use permitted sharing. Instructions and support correspond to the intended population. |
| Authorised caregivers or other helpers, where enabled | Assist the patient or enter permitted information within the configured access and attribution arrangements. |
| Coaches and healthcare professionals (HCPs) | Review information for assigned patients and perform the follow-up activities permitted by their role. A coach role does not itself establish clinical qualifications; activities requiring clinical judgement are assigned to appropriately qualified professionals. |
| Researchers and data managers, where enabled | Access or export the information permitted for the defined research or data-management purpose. Research access does not automatically confer a clinical care role. |
| Administrators | Configure authorised users, assignments, content, instruments, workflows and integrations within their permissions. Administrative access does not establish a clinical role. |

Training and instructions cover the enabled workflow, interpretation of the information shown, limitations and any expected response to notifications.

## 6. Use environment and required equipment

EspHealth is provided through a cloud-hosted web application and mobile interfaces for iOS and Android. Patient use can take place in home or everyday settings; authorised professional users access the relevant interface in their care, coaching or research setting.

Use requires a supported smartphone, tablet or computer, the applicable application or browser, and connectivity for functions that exchange data with the server. Supported operating-system and browser versions, network requirements and any offline behaviour are specified for the covered release.

Paper capture, wearables and other integrations add their own equipment, account, interface and compatibility requirements. The supported combinations are identified for the release/configuration.

## 7. Operating principle

Patients or authorised helpers enter information through the patient interface. Enabled integrations can add paper-derived entries, wearable observations or data from other systems. The application associates information with the relevant patient and source and makes it available through configured views, charts, questionnaire results and authorised outputs.

Configured rules use the relevant input values, scores, profiles or events to generate notifications or select content. The intended source data, calculation or trigger rule, recipient and meaning of each output are specified in the corresponding requirements and configuration records. External analysis results retain a defined source and interpretation.

The input-to-output description and verification account for incomplete, delayed, incorrect or unavailable data and failures in connected services. OCR-derived entries and imported measurements need the review and data-quality handling defined for their use; they are not assumed to be accurate solely because they were imported.

The software has no direct contact with body tissue or bodily fluids. Any physical contact or measurement is associated with the connected hardware and its own intended use.

## 8. Use limitations

This draft does not establish an intended claim for autonomous diagnosis, automatic treatment selection or adjustment, emergency response, or continuous real-time surveillance. The clinical-purpose review confirms whether any such use is proposed; any included use needs an explicit description and supporting evidence.

Displayed information and notifications depend on the available inputs, configured rules, connectivity and user workflow. The expected review interval, recipient responsibilities and response arrangements are defined for the relevant use. Absence of a notification is not evidence that a patient’s condition is stable.

A third-party measurement, questionnaire score or external analytical result retains its own source limitations. Its suitability for the proposed EspHealth use is assessed with the integration and intended population. Educational content and clinical-trial information are described according to their actual purpose; their availability does not establish a treatment recommendation or trial-eligibility decision.

## 9. Configurations, interfaces and accessories

Branding, enabled functions, forms, languages, content, user permissions and integrations can vary between configurations. The configuration record identifies the software release, intended users and population, selected functions and use conditions.

Supported wearables and paper/OCR workflows are identified with their compatible devices, formats and interfaces. The regulatory assessment determines whether an item is an accessory or a separate connected product; a data connection alone does not settle that status.

The qualification/classification assessment considers the functions and interfaces together. A change in branding or access permissions alone does not establish a separate regulatory scope. Changes to clinical claims, target populations, output interpretation or connected analyses are reflected in the intended use and supporting assessment.
